Agent's Take

Here's What I'm Seeing

Analysis · Zachary Springborn · JPAR® Modern Real Estate · Sept. 9, 2026

Chelsea, reviewing the actual documents has changed the picture in two important ways. First and most urgently: the SPR approval has a three-year validity per the determination letter, which means it expired on February 2, 2026. We are now seven months past that date with no indication in any of these documents that an extension was obtained. This has to be verified with Boulder County Community Planning & Permitting before we go any further — it is the threshold question. If the SPR has lapsed, the seller is essentially selling land with infrastructure and the promise of an approval that may no longer be valid. A new SPR application would take months and its outcome cannot be guaranteed at the same square footage.

Second, the well is significantly older than the seller indicated. The seller said "around 2004." State records show the well was drilled September 15, 1993 — 33 years ago. The production rate at the time of drilling was 2 GPM. That's a low number even for a mountain well, and after three decades in granite, the casing and yield need to be independently tested. A 2-GPM well on a property that has been vacant for years could have degraded further, or it could be producing better — there's no way to know without a current test. This is not a deal-stopper, but it is something we need eyes on before any commitment.

The septic was installed in 1994 — the same vintage. The seller correctly represented that it needs to be connected, but calling it "1999" slightly understates the age. It is a 32-year-old absorption bed system. Boulder County Public Health requires an OWTS minor repair permit before a building permit can be issued. That permit process will involve inspection of the existing system. If the absorption field has been compromised over 30 years of non-use or from site activity related to the fire and subsequent clearing, it could require replacement — which is a significant cost on a mountain parcel.

The architect is now identified: Jared Pohl of Applied Architecture, LLC in Pennsylvania. The drawings in the SPR package are professional and detailed. I'd reach out to him directly to understand the current status of the plans, whether any building permit application was filed during the SPR window, and whether he is still engaged with the sellers. That conversation will tell us a lot about how serious the seller is about the built-out path.

The road access is legitimately documented and the assignment to Baker is clean. The BLM right-of-way runs through 2028. The bigger road issue is not legal access — it's the Four Mile FPD's concerns, which are on record in the SPR file and are not going away. Any future permit application will face those same concerns from the fire district, and they'll need to see pullouts, turnarounds, emergency water, and sprinkler plans before sign-off.
